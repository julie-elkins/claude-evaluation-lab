# Teaching strategy integration

The [AI Teaching Strategies repository](https://github.com/julie-elkins/ai-teaching-strategies)
holds reusable teaching and adoption cards for this course. This file keeps the
course-specific application visible without copying the cards or turning course
completion into a learning-impact claim.

## Use the cards in this order

1. **Audience calibration:** identify the learner's decision, prerequisites, and
   minimum path.
2. **Demonstration first:** show one complete synthetic workflow and its
   deliberately degraded case.
3. **Failure-driven labs:** require the learner to inspect the missing evidence,
   terminal state, and next action.
4. **Assessment and feedback:** score the learner's evidence and explanation,
   not only whether a command completed.
5. **Instructor calibration:** use the same observer rubric before another
   facilitator delivers the lesson.
6. **Learner adoption evidence:** use task-level artifacts or bounded practice
   results when deciding what to revise.
7. **Attention-adjusted feedback:** ask for explicit feedback when it is likely
   to change a teaching or evaluation decision enough to justify the learner's
   attention; treat edits, retries, and abandonment as signals that need review,
   not automatic labels.
8. **Take-forward handoff:** close each lesson or lab with `🧭 Take this
   forward`: state what to remember, what to try next, what evidence to inspect,
   and what the example does not prove.

## Course boundary

The course remains offline, synthetic, and provider-neutral on the required
path. The cards do not add live provider calls, learner tracking, or a
leaderboard. A learner's successful lab run shows that the declared exercise
worked; it does not prove general technical competence, production readiness,
or long-term learning.

Keep learner records, private traces, unpublished exam material, and provider
credentials out of this public repository. Store any approved internal
facilitator evidence in its owning private system.
